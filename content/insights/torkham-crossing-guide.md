Torkham is the busiest commercial crossing between Pakistan and Afghanistan. On a good day, cargo moves through in six to eight hours. On a bad day — new tariff dispute, security incident, seasonal congestion — trucks queue for four or five days. This is what we know from running the corridor.

## What you need before the truck rolls

The documents that actually matter, in the order customs looks at them:

- **Commercial invoice** — signed, stamped, with HS codes correct to eight digits.
- **Packing list** — matching the invoice line-by-line. Discrepancies here are the single most common cause of delay.
- **Bill of Lading** or **Transit Declaration** — depending on whether the cargo is inbound to AF or transiting to a third country.
- **Certificate of Origin** — if you want the FTA reduction on the AF end.
- **Vehicle registration + driver documents** — Pakistani plates need onward transit authorization; Afghan-plated trucks need current commercial licence.

Everything in **English or Dari**. Urdu-only paperwork gets rejected at the AF inspection booth.

## The timing that catches people out

Torkham operates commercial hours, but the reality is narrower:

- **Peak throughput** is roughly 07:00–14:00 local. Anything arriving after 12:00 is unlikely to clear the same day.
- **Prayer breaks** on both sides pause processing for ~30 minutes each. Fridays lose an extra hour.
- **Wednesday and Saturday** are the heaviest days. If your ship-by date has any flexibility, aim for a Tuesday arrival.

Plan the truck's Pakistan-side departure so it hits the crossing between 08:00 and 10:00. Anything later stacks up in Peshawar and burns a day.

## What actually causes delays

In our experience, in rough order of frequency:

1. **HS code mismatch** between the shipping documents and what customs classifies the goods as. Almost always a debate about whether an item is "consumer" or "commercial" grade — big tariff swing on the AF side.
2. **Weight or count discrepancies** between the packing list and the physical count on inspection.
3. **Missing exporter's stamp** on the origin certificate — the FTA reduction gets voided.
4. **Truck condition or driver documentation** issues on either side.
5. **Bad-day-at-the-border** issues — political, security or tariff-adjustment events that stop movement entirely.

Only the last category is genuinely outside your control. The other four are all preventable with pre-departure document QA.

## When a truck gets held

The single biggest mistake shippers make is trying to fix things by phone from Karachi or Dubai when the truck's already stopped. It doesn't work. What does:

- A named broker present at the crossing with authority to sign for adjustments.
- Original hard copies of every document, not scans.
- Cash on hand for legitimate expedite fees (they exist and are documented).

If you don't have the first item, the other two don't help. If you're moving regular volume through Torkham, having a named broker on retainer at the crossing pays for itself inside two shipments.
